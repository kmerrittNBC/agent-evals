**No.** On the retry path for the *same candidate*, RelayView does not send a second merge request: it reconciles by reading provider state. That is a guarantee about the coordinator’s dispatch path—not a guarantee that the timed-out request did or did not take effect at the provider.

At pinned RelayView commit `f7c03941cf32b8e25720b5eb1aa91364a236b005`:

- **Admission precedes dispatch.** `src/pr/mergeCoordinator.ts` calls the owner-supplied `verifyAuthority`, then looks up the candidate. For a new candidate, `src/pr/mergeStore.ts` transactionally records a `pending` effect before calling `requestExactMerge`. The record pins the binding/revision, repository and PR, head and base revisions, decision and actor IDs, and effect ID. Migration 077 adds uniqueness on `(binding_id, native_pr_id, head_revision)` (`src/storage/migrations.ts`).
- **The durable states are `pending`, `unknown`, and `confirmed`.** `pending` means intent was admitted, not necessarily that a provider call was sent. Errors or an unestablished outcome become `unknown`; only an accepted matching observation becomes `confirmed`.
- **Confirmation requires an exact observation.** The PR must be observed as `merged`, and both its head revision and merged revision must equal the approved head SHA. If the read is missing, unreadable, or mismatched, the result remains unknown; the coordinator does not treat uncertainty as success.
- **Retry and admission collisions do not resend.** An existing identical intent is reused and takes a read-only observation path. A differing intent for the same candidate key is rejected as an identity conflict. A concurrent admission that wins the unique candidate key likewise sends the other coordinator down the existing-effect path, not to dispatch. This depends on contenders sharing the durable store; it is not a provider-side lock.

The tests support that bounded claim, not provider-level exactly-once semantics. `src/pr/mergeCoordinator.test.ts` asserts that a fake provider timeout leaves the effect unknown, retry keeps the merge-call count at one, and a later exact merged observation confirms it. Its “concurrent admission” case inserts a winning effect before the coordinator call—it does **not** stress simultaneous callers or multiple owner processes. `mergeStore.test.ts` checks identical-intent reuse and conflict rejection; contract tests cover merge preconditions and ambiguous outcomes. The Gitea adapter test checks the request body; the opt-in real-Gitea test calls the adapter directly, not timeout recovery through the coordinator.

So the tests and code establish **“RelayView does not blindly resend this admitted candidate.”** They do not establish whether a timed-out request reached or changed the provider, whether reads are immediately consistent, or what happens under every provider race. If the read cannot confirm the exact merge, RelayView leaves the effect unresolved rather than guessing or retrying the write.
